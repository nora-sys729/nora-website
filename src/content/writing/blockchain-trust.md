I used to think blockchain was mainly about cryptocurrency.

The more I learn about it, the more I think the interesting question is not whether blockchain removes trust — but who we choose to trust instead.

## The problem with centralized systems

Most of the digital systems we use every day depend on intermediaries.

Banks process payments.

Platforms distribute information.

Companies store our data.

Institutions decide which transactions are valid.

This is convenient, but it also means that we place a considerable amount of trust in organizations.

## What blockchain changes

A blockchain creates a shared record that can be maintained by a network rather than a single organization.

Instead of asking:

> "Do I trust this company?"

the system can ask:

> "Can the network verify this?"

That is a very different way of thinking about trust.

## But decentralization is not magic

Blockchain does not eliminate trust completely.

We still trust software, protocols, developers, hardware, exchanges and the people operating the surrounding infrastructure.

The interesting change is that trust can move from a single institution toward a combination of mathematics, code, incentives and distributed verification.

And that may be the more important idea behind blockchain.
